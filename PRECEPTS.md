# The Precepts

> The first utterance of the Dharma Harness: a fixed precept from the Gita, then a rotating
> *precept of the day* from the classical Indian canon — the Bhagavad Gita, the Upanishads,
> the Dhammapada. All in established public-domain translations (Edwin Arnold, Max Müller).

## The opening precept (fixed)

```text
"Let right deeds be thy motive, not the fruit which comes from them."
                            — Bhagavad Gita 2.47, tr. Edwin Arnold
```

## The rotating precept

Beneath the fixed opening, the harness prints one rotating line from [`precepts.txt`](precepts.txt)
— a precept of the day, changing daily. Edit [`precepts.txt`](precepts.txt) (one `Precept — Source`
per line) to curate or extend the pool. All sources are public-domain. The current pool:

> - "This is better, that one do his own task as he may, even though he fail, than take tasks not his own, though they seem good." — Bhagavad Gita 3.35, tr. Edwin Arnold (The Song Celestial)
> - "Know the Self to be sitting in the chariot, the body to be the chariot, the intellect the charioteer, and the mind the reins." — Katha Upanishad 1.3.3, tr. F. Max Müller
> - "All that we are is the result of what we have thought: it is founded on our thoughts, it is made up of our thoughts." — Dhammapada 1, tr. F. Max Müller
> - "If one man conquer in battle a thousand times thousand men, and if another conquer himself, he is the greatest of conquerors." — Dhammapada 103, tr. F. Max Müller
> - "Equability is Yog, is piety!" — Bhagavad Gita 2.48, tr. Edwin Arnold (The Song Celestial)
> - "Steadfast a lamp burns sheltered from the wind; such is the likeness of the Yogi's mind." — Bhagavad Gita 6.19, tr. Edwin Arnold (The Song Celestial)
> - "As a solid rock is not shaken by the wind, wise people falter not amidst blame and praise." — Dhammapada 81, tr. F. Max Müller
> - "The true prevails, not the untrue." — Mundaka Upanishad 3.1.6, tr. F. Max Müller
> - "Speak the truth, do not yield to anger; give, if thou art asked for little." — Dhammapada 224, tr. F. Max Müller
> - "When thou hast surrendered all this, then thou mayest enjoy. Do not covet the wealth of any man!" — Isha Upanishad 1, tr. F. Max Müller
> - "Give up what is before, give up what is behind, give up what is in the middle." — Dhammapada 348, tr. F. Max Müller

*The harness emits this first, on startup — [`bin/precept`](bin/precept).*
